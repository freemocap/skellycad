What changed

USB has a visible path

The P4 turns around so USB leaves toward the connector. Copper now runs through the series resistors and protection device to the four-contact JST.

These are trial paths: differential spacing, length matching and impedance still need work.

Power parts have a purpose

Each regulator gets a nearby inductor and capacitor group. The P4’s 16 designated small decouplers move onto the back near the pins they serve.

Switch to the back view to see them. Their power and ground vias are not routed yet.

The camera gets an exit

Six short traces leave the P4 for its three camera-signal pairs. The upper corridor and right-hand connector budget leave space to continue them.

The 14 × 14 mm sensor head is preserved from R03. Flex and optics remain unselected.

This is a partial routing study, not a board to order. The controller still has 171 unconnected items. DRC reports the six deliberate open camera-trace ends, with no other violation types under the candidate 0.15 mm clearance rule. Power planes, decoupling vias, full camera routing and physical imaging tests remain unfinished.

The finer rule replaces R03’s default 0.20 mm clearance. It is documented as a candidate process assumption; it is not a fabrication approval.

Measurements and checks

All 99 footprints remain accounted for; 408 pad-net assignments match the source circuit. No same-side component bounding boxes overlap. All 22 pad-to-pad routes are connected in the saved copper graph. Schematic ERC: zero errors and warnings.

The median projected distance from each designated decoupler pad to its P4 power pin changes from 13.33 mm to 0.13 mm. This is XY placement only: opposite-side components still need vias and ground connections.

USB copper lengths and their mismatch are recorded in validation.json. The routes are not a length-matched differential pair. The overall flattened assembly remains 76 mm long.
