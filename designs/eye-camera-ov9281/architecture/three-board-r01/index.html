Four capture buffers plus one USB staging frame require 1.28 MB at 640 × 400. Two spare frames provide only 20 ms of stall tolerance at 100 fps. The candidate CSI rates are compatible on paper; firmware and memory bandwidth still need measurements. No end-to-end latency claim is made.

Power through the tether

Nominal delivered head power is about 0.63 W. The 0.9 W higher-load scenario is an explicit engineering screen, not a required “double everything” standard.

At 4.75 V source, 40 °C conductor and 0.9 W load, modeled head input is 4.52 V at 30 cm and 4.30 V at 60 cm. This passes the chosen 3.3 V input screen. It does not qualify cable signal integrity.

Keep the hot work off the animal

The serializer alone contributes approximately 0.28 W typical [@ti953A01]. Removing the ESP32 helps, but the local assembly still needs thermal testing. No safe surface temperature is inferred from its mass.

The regulated bench power allocation is 5 V / 1 A. Product USB power negotiation, protection and backfeed isolation remain design tasks.

What still prevents a build order?

  1. Sensor supply: exact OV09281-H64A listings conflict; no traceable immediately orderable supply was verified. Procurement evidence and review scope.
  2. Cable and connector: no qualified 36 AWG high-speed channel yet. Four contacts alone do not establish compatibility.
  3. Electrical and firmware detail: pin-complete circuits, clock/reset/control audit, continuous capture and measured USB throughput.
  4. Optics: an actual tiny IR-pass lens/holder with focus at 10–20 mm, image coverage, retention and confirmed mass.
  5. Physical qualification: temperatures, illumination, bending, mounting, assembly mass and manufacturer-approved routing/stack.

Recommended next phase: secure the exact sensor/P4 sourcing path, turn the architecture into pin-complete schematics, and define a reproducible serial-link/USB bench test. Then route the compact islands using qualified parts. Continue reducing mass where the evidence supports it, without calling the present envelopes a finished board.
